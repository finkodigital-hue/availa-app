# Gift redemption and booking refund verification — 24 September 2026

Gift redemption now explicitly updates both paid and due amounts. It rejects expired cards, currency mismatches, cancelled/refunded bookings and unresolved balance checkouts. An idempotency replay must match its booking, card and actor. Database locks serialise booking balance and card changes.

Confirmed booking refunds may be partial. Each provider refund is applied once and must match its original charge, business, booking, currency and remaining refundable amount. The website requests only outstanding charge balances, keeps partially refunded charges eligible, and reports requests as submitted rather than claiming bank delivery. The server records failed attempts after owner authorisation; authenticated clients still cannot forge payment rows.

The signed webhook accepts `refund.created` and `refund.updated`. It resolves dashboard-originated refunds through the successful charge within the signed connected account; metadata cannot redirect it to another booking. Missing charges/database failures return 500 for retry instead of silently dropping reconciliation. Unfulfilled-booking recovery retains its separate durable process.

Evidence: 25 payment-ledger assertions run against all application migrations; eight refund-balance assertions; 15 actual-handler assertions using fictional signed events and a mocked database. No test initiates a provider payment, refund, booking or message. TypeScript, production compilation and the security boundary check pass. These fixtures do not prove real Stripe delivery, concurrent independent database sessions or the complete refund lifecycle.

Gift-purchase refund follow-up: confirmed refunds now remove the corresponding unspent value atomically and add a refund record and credit ledger entry. Partial refunds preserve the remaining credit; full refunds void an unused card. Refunding already-spent value removes any remaining balance and creates a manual-review flag visible to the owner and the protected monitor. Duplicate identity/currency/amount mismatches and refunds exceeding the purchase are rejected. A refund arriving before purchase reconciliation receives a retryable error. Eighteen full-schema assertions cover these paths and owner/cross-tenant/function permissions; four additional signed-handler assertions cover gift routing.

The booking-refund release was verified live at 57af0c1a7c504f075cd0de23f8a9618b8dfa00e1. After deployment, the connected sandbox webhook was successfully subscribed to `refund.created`, preserving its existing events.

Refund failure follow-up (26 September): signed failed, cancelled and action-required refund notifications create a persistent owner-visible review record and a protected-monitor alert. The private function binds the review to an existing purchase within the signed account; duplicate notifications cannot change that identity. Twelve full-schema assertions and nine additional signed-handler assertions cover this path. The alert does not automatically reverse the ledger, restore gift credit or charge the customer. An operator must compare current Stripe state and reconcile before clearing it. The connected endpoint should also subscribe to `refund.failed`; confirm provider configuration after deployment.

Still open: provider sandbox end-to-end journeys, operational alert receipt and owner reconciliation of historical payments and refund reversals. Gift refunds against spent credit require an operator to reconcile the original purchase and associated bookings; the code deliberately does not invent a debt or charge the customer. Do not mark commercial payments cleared from these tests alone.

References: [Stripe refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds) and [event types](https://docs.stripe.com/api/events/types). Refunds can remain pending or later fail; a submitted request is not final delivery.
